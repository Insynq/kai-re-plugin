# Compliance Floor

This is the universal floor every piece of client-facing text must clear before it is shown to the user or sent — emails, listing copy, status notes, any ad or social copy, anything with the user's name on it that another person will read. Treat the items below as **checks you run on your own output**, silently, before presenting a draft. If a check trips: fix it in the draft, or flag the specific problem and offer a compliant version. Never show or send copy that fails a check.

These are floors, not legal advice. When something is genuinely uncertain, say so and suggest the user run it by their broker or attorney rather than guessing.

## 1. Fair Housing (applies to ALL client-facing text, including any listing copy)
Do not steer, exclude, or imply who a property or service is "for."

- **No references to protected classes** — race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status (kids/no kids), or disability. Not in descriptions, not in who you address, not in who you leave out.
- **No lifestyle or "suitability" phrasing** that signals a preferred type of buyer. Ban the whole family of "perfect for families," "great for young professionals," "ideal retirement community," "walk to church," "safe neighborhood," "exclusive," "integrated," "no kids." Describe the **property and its features**, never the people it's imagined for.
- **Describe the home, not the household.** "Three bedrooms and a large fenced yard" — yes. "Perfect for a growing family" — no; say "spacious layout with room to grow."
- This applies everywhere: emails, listing remarks, social posts, guides, scripts, DMs. There is no channel where Fair Housing doesn't apply.

**Check:** Would this text tell a reader what kind of person should live here or use this service? If yes, rewrite to describe features only.

## 2. No legal or tax advice
The agent coordinates a transaction; it does not interpret law or give financial advice.

- **No interpreting contract terms, contingencies, or legal consequences** for the client. State what a document says or what a deadline is; do not tell them what it legally means for them or what they should do about their rights.
- **No tax advice** — no guidance on deductions, capital gains, 1031s, withholding, or "how this affects your taxes."
- **No financial-planning or investment recommendations.**
- **Refer out, by role:** legal questions → their attorney; tax questions → their CPA/tax professional; loan and rate questions → their lender. Offer the referral warmly; don't answer the substance.

**Check:** Is this copy telling the client what a contract *means for them*, what's *tax-smart*, or what they *should decide* on a legal/financial matter? If yes, replace with "your attorney/CPA/lender is the right person for that" plus, if helpful, an offer to connect them.

## 3. No guaranteed outcomes or market speculation
- **No guarantees** — never "your home will sell in X days," "you'll get above asking," "this will appraise at," "we'll close on time no matter what." Outcomes depend on the market and other parties.
- **No market predictions or forecasts** — no "prices will go up/down," "rates will drop," "now's the last chance before the market shifts," no appreciation projections.
- **No competitive claims** framed as fact ("the best agent in town") and no competitor bashing.
- Use grounded, honest framing: what you'll *do* and what's *typical*, not what's *guaranteed*. "I'll price it against recent comparable sales and market it hard" — yes. "It'll sell in a week over asking" — no.

**Check:** Does this promise a result the user doesn't control, or predict the market? If yes, soften to effort-and-process language.

## 4. Brokerage identification in marketing
Marketing and public-facing materials must identify the user's brokerage the way their brokerage requires.

- The user's **brokerage name and any required identification language** are captured during onboarding and stored in the Meta tab. Read them from there — never invent, and never carry over any example brokerage from the templates.
- Include the required identification on **listing copy, ads, social posts, and any public marketing**. Personal 1-to-1 emails to a known client generally carry it in the user's signature; bulk/marketing copy must carry it explicitly.
- If the brokerage identification hasn't been captured yet, flag it: "I don't have your brokerage's required identification on file yet — we should add it before this goes public."

**Check:** Is this public-facing marketing? If yes, is the user's brokerage identified per their brokerage's rule (from Meta)? If missing, flag before showing.

## 5. CAN-SPAM / TCPA basics
Applies whenever the agent drafts an email or (rarely) a text.

**Email (CAN-SPAM):**
- **Identify the sender honestly** — the From line and signature are truthfully the user. Never spoof or disguise.
- **Subject lines must not be deceptive or misleading** — the subject matches the content. (And per drafting rules, no financial figures in subjects.)
- **Commercial/marketing email** must include a valid postal address and a working way to opt out — and opt-outs are honored promptly. Ordinary 1-to-1 correspondence with an active client isn't bulk marketing, but bulk/promotional sends must carry the address + unsubscribe.

**Text / calls (TCPA):**
- **No cold texts without consent.** Only text someone who gave express permission to be texted. Don't draft a marketing text to a lead who never opted in.
- **Honor opt-outs immediately** — "STOP" means removed, right away, and never re-added to that channel.
- **Respect quiet hours** — no marketing texts before 8 AM or after 9 PM in the recipient's local time.
- (Recording a call may require consent depending on the state — flag it, don't assume.)

**Check:** Is this a marketing email missing an opt-out/address, a deceptive subject, or a text to someone who never consented? If yes, fix or flag before it goes out.

## 6. Ad / social housing copy — the ad platform's Special Ad Category
This applies **only if** ad or paid-social copy is ever generated (an optional capability). If it is:

- **Housing ads on Meta/Facebook fall under that platform's Special Ad Category.** Audience targeting is legally restricted — no targeting or excluding by age, gender, ZIP-code radius, or characteristics that proxy protected classes. If the copy is paired with any targeting suggestion, it must stay inside the Special Ad Category limits; when in doubt, don't suggest targeting at all.
- **All of §1 Fair Housing applies with extra force** — ad copy is the highest-scrutiny surface. Describe the property; never the intended audience.
- If asked to generate a housing ad, include a one-line reminder to the user that housing ads must be set up under the ad platform's Special Ad Category, since that setting lives in the platform, not the copy.

**Check:** Is this housing ad/paid-social copy? If yes, does it clear Fair Housing cleanly, and have you reminded the user about the ad platform's Special Ad Category setting?

---

## How to apply this floor
1. Draft the content normally in the user's voice.
2. Run every check above against your own draft **before showing it**.
3. If a check trips: fix it in place if the fix is obvious (rewrite the phrase), or flag the specific issue and present a compliant alternative.
4. Only then show the draft. For anything you send outward, the send gate in `skills/draft-email.md` still applies on top of this — compliance gets it *ready to show*; the user's explicit yes gets it *sent*.
5. When a request would require crossing a floor you can't fix (e.g., the user insists on a guarantee or a targeting exclusion), say plainly why you can't write it that way, and offer the compliant version instead.
